The overall picture

Buying shares in a Bangladesh company involves several connected processes. The parties agree their commercial bargain, the company records the change of ownership, and the applicable tax, regulatory and foreign-exchange requirements must be satisfied. A signed share purchase agreement is an important milestone, but it does not replace the transfer instrument, company records or regulatory steps.

This guide concerns an ordinary sale of existing shares in a private or unlisted public company incorporated in Bangladesh, with a foreign investor on one or both sides. A new share issue, listed securities transaction, capital reduction or acquisition of an overseas holding company requires a different analysis.

Start with the right transaction route

Establish whether each party is resident or non-resident under the applicable foreign-exchange rules. Citizenship alone does not answer that question; tax residence is also a separate enquiry. Identify the seller, the buyer, the company whose shares are changing hands, and where the consideration will be paid.

An Authorised Dealer (AD) is a bank authorised to conduct foreign-exchange business. Bring the AD into the transaction early. Bangladesh Bank’s FEID Circular No. 01 of 8 March 2026 sets out the following framework for the unlisted-company transactions within its scope. [1]

Who sells to whom?Starting point under the 2026 circular
Resident → non-residentThe AD may facilitate the transfer without prior Bangladesh Bank approval, subject to fair value and the circular’s conditions. For a deal of up to BDT 10 million, a joint price-justification statement replaces an independent valuation report; above that, a report is required.
Non-resident → non-residentThe same fair-value and valuation-document framework applies. Payment outside Bangladesh does not remove local transfer, tax or reporting obligations. Agree the settlement evidence with the AD.
Non-resident → residentThe AD can process sale proceeds without prior Bangladesh Bank approval through specified routes: qualifying NAV-based pricing regardless of amount; deals up to BDT 10 million with joint price justification; or deals up to BDT 1 billion supported by the prescribed independent valuation. Cases outside those routes go to Bangladesh Bank through the AD.
Resident → residentCompany-law, contractual and tax requirements still apply. A wholly domestic sale does not become a foreign-investment exit merely because the company has other foreign shareholders.

Read the conditions with the thresholds. BDT 10 million is Tk 1 crore; BDT 1 billion is Tk 100 crore. The NAV route has specific accounting and auditor requirements. The thresholds are not unconditional exemptions from KYC, tax, documentation or other approvals. Bangladesh Bank and the AD retain valuation-review powers.

Before signing: check what can actually be sold

Begin with the company’s constitutional documents, register of members, share certificates and RJSC records. Reconcile the seller’s name, share numbers, class, paid-up status and holding. Investigate gaps in past allotments or transfers, pledges, charges, competing claims and any mismatch between records.

The articles of association and shareholders’ agreement may contain pre-emption rights, consent requirements or restrictions on transfer. Obtain the required waivers and corporate authorisations. Section 38 preserves the company’s power to refuse registration under its articles; private-company shares should not be treated as freely tradeable without checking those provisions. [2]

Review the business as well as the shares: financing documents, key contracts, land and leases, licences, tax exposure, employment matters and disputes. A change of shareholders usually leaves the same company holding its assets and liabilities. That continuity makes due diligence and contractual protection especially important.

Prepare an approvals matrix for the actual business. Sector rules, licence conditions, lender consents, competition considerations and investment-zone requirements may affect the deal. Do not assume that a permission relating to one aspect of the transaction clears every other requirement.

Turn the legal requirements into a closing plan

The share purchase agreement (SPA) should identify the shares, price, payment arrangements, conditions to closing, warranties, indemnities and closing deliverables. Allocate responsibility for tax, stamp duty and filing costs. Agree who controls the originals and what evidence permits funds to be released. The SPA and the AD’s required sale-purchase memorandum should be consistent.

Prepare the documents—and the signing route

Form 117 is the prescribed instrument of transfer of shares. It performs a different function from the SPA. Section 38(3) requires a properly stamped transfer instrument executed by both transferor and transferee, delivered to the company with the relevant share certificate, subject to the statutory exceptions. [2] [3]

A typical execution pack includes Form 117, the seller’s supporting affidavit, corporate resolutions authorising the signatories, the target company’s transfer resolution, share certificates and identification documents. Check the current RJSC checklist before circulating originals. Arrange witnessed signatures and the original instrument; do not assume the SPA’s counterpart or electronic-signing provisions also work for Form 117.

If the seller signs in Bangladesh

Section 38(3A) provides for the transferor’s personal appearance and re-signing before the Registrar after the specified documents have been submitted. For a corporate seller, settle the authorised representative’s authority, identification and attendance arrangements with RJSC in advance. A Registrar’s commission is a statutory alternative where justified, subject to the prescribed fee; it is not an automatic substitute for attendance. [2]

If the seller is abroad

The same provision contains an embassy-authentication route for a foreign citizen or transferor abroad. Plan notarisation, authentication, any required local validation, translations and courier delivery before fixing the closing date. A notarised scan should not be assumed sufficient.

There is a further development to check: the Apostille Convention entered into force for Bangladesh on 30 March 2025. Whether an apostille can replace legalisation depends on the originating country, the document and the Convention’s operation between the countries—including any objection to Bangladesh’s accession. Confirm the treatment of the actual document with the receiving authority. “All overseas documents need consular legalisation” and “an apostille always works” are both unsafe assumptions. [4]

Align the price, tax and banking arrangements

Commercial price and regulatory value

A negotiated price does not automatically establish the amount acceptable for foreign-exchange purposes. Where required, the valuation must follow the circular’s prescribed methods and supporting requirements. Its recognised approaches include net assets, market comparables and discounted cash flow, used with a justified methodology. Commission that work early enough to influence the deal timetable. [1]

Three dates to put into the transaction calendar

6 months
The latest audited accounts used at the dated sale-purchase MoU must not be older than six months; interim audited accounts are needed if they are.
45 days
The circular requires the transfer procedure to finish within 45 days of the MoU or Bangladesh Bank approval, whichever is later.
14 days
The AD’s post-facto report is due within 14 days of transfer. Assign responsibility and assemble the evidence before closing.

FEID Circular No. 01, paragraphs 2(b)–(c) and 3. These are transaction requirements, not a promise that every deal will close within that period.

Stamp duty is separate from capital gains tax

The statutory share-transfer entry in Schedule I, as substituted by the Finance Act 2022, specifies 1.5% of the consideration. Use that published entry as a starting point for the duty review, then confirm the currently applicable rate, valuation basis, exemptions and payment procedure for the instrument at execution. Budget separately for the SPA, affidavit, certificates, filing and authentication costs where applicable. [5]

Income tax requires its own analysis. The Income Tax Act treats shares in a Bangladesh-resident company as property in Bangladesh, and also addresses certain indirect offshore transfers. An offshore seller or offshore payment does not, by itself, eliminate Bangladesh tax. Section 119(4) prevents effecting a share transfer where capital gains arise for a non-resident until the tax payable on those gains has been paid. Treaty relief and any NBR certificate should be dealt with through the applicable procedure, rather than assumed from the seller’s nationality. [6]

Do not apply one capital-gains percentage to every investor. The seller’s status, transaction, applicable year, amendments and any treaty entitlement affect the analysis. Establish the tax computation, withholding or collection responsibility, payment evidence and any relief before the transfer is completed.

The 2026 Bangladesh Bank circular also requires a non-resident’s applicable gain tax and stamp duty to be funded through money brought from abroad via the banking channel; the target company must not fund those amounts. Arrange the funding and payment trail with the AD rather than assuming that a local company can advance the money. [1, para. 2(h)]

Close the deal and record the new ownership

Use an agreed sequence for satisfied conditions, required permissions, executed documents, duty and tax evidence, release of money and company action. If escrow is proposed, check the banking route first: the circular contemplates crediting sale proceeds to escrow with prior Bangladesh Bank permission. Do not introduce it as a last-minute workaround. [1]

The company should approve and record the transfer in accordance with its articles and the law, update its register of members and transfer records, and make the appropriate share-certificate endorsements or replacements. Arrange the company’s certification of the transfer instrument where required. Section 39 addresses that certification; it is not an assurance that the seller has an indefeasible title. [7]

Complete the current RJSC submission and signature-verification process with the executed instrument, affidavit, authority documents, company resolutions, duty evidence and other required records. Coordinate online submissions with the delivery and verification of originals. A portal upload alone is not a reliable closing test.

Keep the concepts distinct: contractual completion, registration in the company’s records, RJSC processing and bank settlement are connected milestones. The closing checklist should specify the evidence required for each, rather than relying on an informal statement that “the shares have been transferred”.

Finish the post-transfer work

Under paragraph 3 of the 2026 circular, the AD must report the relevant transfer to Bangladesh Bank within 14 days, supported by the RJSC-endorsed Form 117, up-to-date Schedule X, banking evidence with the FX transaction ID, the sale-purchase MoU, valuation where applicable and the relevant committee records. Agree how current shareholder information and the reporting documents will be supplied in time; do not simply wait for the next annual return. [1]

If directors change, complete the separate director-consent and particulars filings, including the appropriate Forms IX and XII. Update bank mandates and assess notifications or amendments needed for licences, investment registrations and material contracts. Reflect the ownership change in the company’s subsequent statutory returns. A director-change filing does not substitute for the share-transfer documentation. [3]

Where sale proceeds are to be remitted, the AD will also check the earlier investment and transfer history. Preserve evidence of inward funds, previous share issuances and foreign-exchange reporting. A missing historical record can become an exit problem years after the original investment.

The circular provides a five-working-day AD processing period for qualifying repatriations without observations, deviations or discrepancies. That is not a five-day guarantee for the entire acquisition or for an application requiring Bangladesh Bank approval. [1, paras. 2(d)–(e)]

A practical closing checklist

Give each item an owner and a due date. Adapt the list to the actual company and transaction.

  • Ownership: reconciled company and RJSC records, share certificates, paid-up status and any encumbrance releases.
  • Authority and consents: articles, shareholder rights, waivers, party resolutions, signatory authority and required third-party or regulatory consents.
  • Commercial documents: signed SPA and consistent AD documentation, conditions-to-closing record and agreed funds flow.
  • Execution: Form 117, affidavit, witnessed signatures, authentication or attendance arrangements, and originals ready for filing.
  • Value and tax: current audited accounts, valuation or permitted joint justification, tax analysis, relief evidence where applicable, duty and tax payment receipts.
  • Company and RJSC records: transfer resolution, updated registers, share certificates, required certification and evidence of completed submissions.
  • Banking and follow-through: KYC and ownership information, remittance evidence, prior investment records, 14-day reporting responsibility and any director or licence updates.

Questions international investors often ask

Can the entire deal be completed from overseas?

It may be possible to arrange an overseas execution route, but the formalities depend on the documents, parties and jurisdictions. Confirm authentication, original-document delivery, representation and RJSC requirements before making travel or closing assumptions.

Does every foreign-investor share sale need prior Bangladesh Bank approval?

No. The 2026 circular provides specified AD-handled routes. However, “no prior approval” does not mean “no conditions” or “no reporting”. Identify the correct route before fixing the payment mechanics.

How long should we allow?

Build the timetable around the slowest dependency: consents, current accounts, valuation, tax relief, document authentication, RJSC arrangements or bank review. Do not use an estimated registry-processing period as the timetable for the whole transaction.

Is a share purchase the same as buying the company’s assets?

No. A share buyer acquires an interest in the company. The company remains the owner of its assets and the bearer of its liabilities. An asset purchase has a different transfer, consent and tax analysis.

The most useful starting document is a closing plan that joins the legal steps to the flow of funds. When each required document, decision and payment has a named owner, a cross-border transaction becomes much easier to manage.

Sources and scope

This guide summarises the sources below and practical transaction planning. It is not exhaustive. Check subsequent amendments, current administrative practice and the requirements of the receiving bank or authority before acting.

  1. Bangladesh Bank, FEID Circular No. 01, 8 March 2026 — paragraphs 1–3, valuation requirements in paragraph 5 and the annexures. Unlisted-company transfer and repatriation framework.
  2. Companies Act 1994, section 38 — transfer instrument, signature verification, company registration and articles-based restrictions, including subsection (3A) inserted in 2020.
  3. RJSC, Form 117 and RJSC filing guidance — read with the current submission checklist.
  4. HCCH, entry into force for Bangladesh, Convention status table and objections concerning Bangladesh’s accession — check the country pair and document.
  5. Finance Act 2022, section 3, substituted Stamp Act Schedule I, entry 61(a) — published share-transfer duty entry. Also consult the Stamp Act and subsequent fiscal amendments for the instrument’s execution date.
  6. Income Tax Act 2023, authentic English text published in October 2025 — sections 26–27 and 119. Read with subsequent income-tax amendments, applicable finance legislation, rules and any relevant treaty. No universal capital-gains rate is asserted here.
  7. Companies Act 1994, section 39 — certification of transfers and its legal effect.
