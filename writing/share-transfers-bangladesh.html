The overall picture

To complete a share transfer in Bangladesh, the parties must do more than sign a sale agreement. They must also complete the transfer documents, record the new ownership and meet the applicable tax and banking requirements. Planning these steps together helps avoid a deal that is signed but cannot be completed or paid for.

This guide explains the sale of existing shares in a private or unlisted public company incorporated in Bangladesh, where the buyer, seller or both are foreign investors. It does not cover new share issues, listed securities, capital reductions or purchases of an overseas holding company.

The buyer and seller agree and document the sale. The company records the ownership change and handles company formalities. The bank handles the applicable foreign-exchange checks and reporting. The Registrar of Joint Stock Companies and Firms (RJSC) carries out the relevant registry formalities.

Identify the buyer, seller and payment route

The applicable foreign-exchange route depends on whether the buyer and seller are resident or non-resident. Establish their status under the foreign-exchange rules. Citizenship alone does not decide this, and tax residence is a separate question. Identify the company whose shares are being sold and where the buyer will pay.

An Authorised Dealer (AD) is a bank authorised to conduct foreign-exchange business. Ask the AD to confirm the route early. Bangladesh Bank’s FEID Circular No. 01 of 8 March 2026 provides the following framework for unlisted-company transactions within its scope. [1]

Who sells to whom?Starting point under the 2026 circular
Resident → non-residentThe AD may handle the transfer without prior Bangladesh Bank approval if it meets the fair-value and other conditions. For deals up to BDT 10 million, the parties may submit a joint statement justifying the price instead of an independent valuation. Above that amount, an independent valuation report is required.
Non-resident → non-residentThe same fair-value and valuation requirements apply. Paying outside Bangladesh does not remove local transfer, tax or reporting obligations. Agree the evidence of payment with the AD.
Non-resident → residentThe AD may send sale proceeds abroad without prior Bangladesh Bank approval under three specified routes:
  1. Qualifying net asset value (NAV) pricing, regardless of amount.
  2. Deals up to BDT 10 million with joint price justification.
  3. Deals up to BDT 1 billion with the prescribed independent valuation.
Other cases require Bangladesh Bank approval through the AD.
Resident → residentCompany-law, contractual and tax requirements still apply. Other foreign shareholders in the company do not turn a wholly domestic sale into a foreign-investor exit.

BDT 10 million is Tk 1 crore; BDT 1 billion is Tk 100 crore. NAV broadly means assets less liabilities. The NAV route has specific accounting and auditor requirements.

Each route has conditions beyond the amount involved. Check the documentation, tax, other approvals and the bank’s know-your-customer (KYC) requirements. Bangladesh Bank and the AD may review the valuation.

Before signing: check the shares and the business

First confirm that the seller owns the shares and can transfer them. Compare the company’s governing documents, register of members, share certificates and RJSC records. Check the seller’s name, share numbers, class, holding and how much has been paid on the shares. Resolve gaps in earlier share issues or transfers, pledges, charges, competing claims and inconsistent records.

The articles of association and shareholders’ agreement may restrict transfers or require consent. They may also give existing shareholders a first right to buy, known as a pre-emption right. Obtain the necessary waivers and corporate approvals. Section 38 preserves the company’s power to refuse registration under its articles. [2]

Review the business too: loans, key contracts, land and leases, licences, tax, employment matters and disputes. After a share sale, the same company usually retains its assets and liabilities. The buyer therefore needs to assess those liabilities and agree suitable contractual protection.

List the approvals the particular business needs. Sector rules, licence conditions, lenders, competition requirements and investment-zone rules may affect the sale. Approval for one purpose does not cover every other requirement.

Agree who will do what before closing

The share purchase agreement (SPA) records the commercial terms. It should identify the shares, price, payment method and conditions the parties must meet before completing the sale, commonly called closing. It should also set out warranties, indemnities and the documents each party must deliver.

Warranties are contractual assurances; indemnities allocate specified risks or losses. Their wording matters. The parties should also agree who pays tax, stamp duty and filing costs, who holds the originals, and what permits payment to be released. Keep the SPA consistent with the sale-purchase memorandum of understanding (MoU) required by the AD.

Prepare the documents and arrange signing

Form 117 is the prescribed share-transfer instrument. It is separate from the SPA. Subject to statutory exceptions, section 38(3) requires the seller and buyer to sign a properly stamped transfer instrument. They must deliver it to the company with the relevant share certificate. [2] [3]

The signing documents typically include Form 117, the seller’s affidavit, resolutions authorising the signatories, the company’s transfer resolution, share certificates and identification. An affidavit is a sworn statement. Check RJSC’s current checklist before sending documents for signature.

Arrange witnessed signatures and the original instrument. Permission to sign the SPA electronically or in separate copies does not necessarily apply to Form 117.

If the seller signs in Bangladesh

Section 38(3A) requires the seller to appear and re-sign before the Registrar after the specified documents are submitted. For a corporate seller, agree the authorised representative’s powers, identification and attendance arrangements with RJSC beforehand.

The law also allows a Registrar’s commission where justified, subject to the prescribed fee. Confirm whether that alternative is available for the particular seller; it does not automatically replace attendance. [2]

If the seller is abroad

The same provision allows embassy authentication for a foreign citizen or seller abroad. Confirm the required notarisation, authentication, any local validation and translations. Allow time to courier the originals before fixing the closing date. A notarised scan may not be enough.

The Apostille Convention entered into force for Bangladesh on 30 March 2025. An apostille is a certificate that can replace consular legalisation for documents covered by the Convention. Its use depends on the document and whether the Convention operates between the relevant countries. Some countries have objected to Bangladesh’s accession.

Confirm the receiving authority’s requirements for the actual document. Do not assume that every overseas document needs consular legalisation, or that an apostille always replaces it. [4]

Settle valuation, tax and payment arrangements

Check whether the agreed price meets the banking rules

The price the parties agree may differ from the value the AD can accept. Where a valuation is required, the valuer must follow the circular’s methods and supporting requirements. Recognised approaches include net assets, market comparables and discounted cash flow. These assess assets, comparable businesses or expected future cash flows. The valuer must justify the method used. Arrange the valuation early. [1]

Three time limits to plan for

6 months
At the date of the sale-purchase MoU, the latest audited accounts must be no more than six months old. If they are older, obtain interim audited accounts.
45 days
The circular requires completion of the transfer procedure within 45 days of the MoU or Bangladesh Bank approval, whichever is later.
14 days
The AD must report the transfer within 14 days of transfer. Agree who will supply the supporting documents before closing.

FEID Circular No. 01, paragraphs 2(b)–(c) and 3. These are requirements under the circular, not guaranteed processing times.

Calculate stamp duty and capital gains tax separately

The share-transfer entry in Schedule I, as substituted by the Finance Act 2022, specifies duty at 1.5% of the consideration—the price paid or payable for the shares. Check the rate, valuation basis, exemptions and payment procedure applicable when the instrument is signed. Allow separately for costs relating to the SPA, affidavit, certificates, filing and authentication. [5]

An overseas seller or payment abroad does not, by itself, remove Bangladesh tax. The Income Tax Act treats shares in a Bangladesh-resident company as property in Bangladesh. It also addresses certain indirect transfers through overseas companies.

Section 119(4) requires payment of the tax payable on a non-resident’s capital gains before the share transfer is effected. Any treaty relief or certificate from the National Board of Revenue (NBR) must follow the applicable procedure. The seller’s nationality alone does not establish an entitlement to relief. [6]

The tax rate depends on the seller’s status, transaction, applicable year, amendments and any treaty entitlement. Before completing the transfer, establish the tax calculation, who must withhold or collect it, and the evidence of payment or relief.

The 2026 Bangladesh Bank circular requires a non-resident’s applicable gains tax and stamp duty to be funded from abroad through banking channels. The company whose shares are being sold must not fund those amounts. Agree how the funds will arrive and how payment will be documented with the AD. [1, para. 2(h)]

Complete the sale and record the new owner

Agree the closing sequence before anyone releases documents or money. Identify when the parties will confirm the conditions and approvals, sign, provide duty and tax receipts, release payment and update company records.

If the parties propose an escrow arrangement to hold funds pending agreed conditions, check the banking requirements first. The circular requires prior Bangladesh Bank permission to credit sale proceeds to escrow. [1]

The company should approve and record the transfer under its articles and the law. It should update its register of members and transfer records, then endorse or replace the share certificates as appropriate. Arrange company certification of the transfer instrument where required. Section 39 governs that certification; it does not guarantee the seller’s title to the shares. [7]

Submit the documents RJSC currently requires and complete its signature-verification process. The documents may include the signed instrument, affidavit, authority documents, company resolutions and duty evidence. Coordinate online filing with delivery and verification of the originals.

Track four separate milestones: completion under the contract, registration in the company’s records, RJSC processing and bank settlement. Specify the evidence needed for each. An online upload alone does not establish that all four are complete.

Complete the reporting and other updates

The AD must report the relevant transfer to Bangladesh Bank within 14 days of transfer. Paragraph 3 of the 2026 circular requires supporting documents, including:

  • RJSC-endorsed Form 117 and an up-to-date Schedule X;
  • banking evidence with the foreign-exchange transaction ID;
  • the sale-purchase MoU;
  • the valuation, where applicable, and relevant committee records.

Agree how the company and parties will supply these records on time. Do not wait for the next annual return. [1]

If directors change, complete the separate consent and particulars filings, including the appropriate Forms IX and XII. Update bank mandates. Check whether licences, investment registrations or key contracts require notices or amendments. Record the ownership change in subsequent statutory returns. Director-change filings do not replace share-transfer documents. [3]

Keep the records needed to send sale proceeds abroad

The AD will check the earlier investment and transfer history before processing the remittance. Keep evidence of funds brought into Bangladesh, earlier share issues and foreign-exchange reporting. Missing records can delay an investor’s exit years later.

The circular gives the AD five working days to process qualifying repatriations without observations, deviations or discrepancies. That period does not cover the entire acquisition or an application requiring Bangladesh Bank approval. [1, paras. 2(d)–(e)]

Closing checklist: assign a person and a date

Adapt this checklist to the company and transaction. Give each task a named person and a deadline.

  • Check ownership: reconcile company records, RJSC records and share certificates. Confirm paid-up status and releases of any security over the shares.
  • Obtain authority and consent: check the articles, shareholder rights, waivers, resolutions, signatory powers and required third-party or regulatory consents.
  • Agree the sale and payment terms: sign the SPA and consistent AD documents. Record fulfilment of the closing conditions and agree how payments will move.
  • Arrange signing: prepare Form 117, the affidavit, witnessed signatures, authentication or attendance arrangements, and originals for filing.
  • Complete valuation and tax work: obtain current audited accounts and a valuation or permitted joint price justification. Confirm tax treatment and retain evidence of relief, duty and tax payments.
  • Update company and RJSC records: obtain the transfer resolution, update registers and certificates, and complete required certification and submissions.
  • Finish bank reporting and other updates: provide KYC and ownership information, payment evidence and prior investment records. Assign the 14-day reporting tasks and complete any director or licence updates.

Questions foreign investors often ask

Can the parties complete the sale from overseas?

It may be possible. The signing and authentication requirements depend on the documents, parties and countries involved. Confirm original-document delivery, representation and RJSC formalities before making travel plans or fixing a closing date.

Does every sale involving a foreign investor need prior Bangladesh Bank approval?

No. The 2026 circular allows the AD to handle specified transactions without prior approval. The parties must still meet the relevant conditions, and reporting remains necessary. Confirm the route before agreeing how the buyer will pay.

How long will the transfer take?

The timetable depends on the steps still outstanding. These may include consents, current accounts, valuation, tax relief, document authentication, RJSC formalities or bank review. An estimate for registry processing alone does not cover the whole transaction.

Is buying shares the same as buying the company’s assets?

No. The buyer acquires shares in the company. The company retains its assets and liabilities. Buying assets directly involves different transfer, consent and tax requirements.

Before signing, agree a closing plan with the advisers, company and bank. It should identify who supplies each document, when payment can be released and who completes the reporting.

Sources and scope

This guide explains the sources below and practical steps for planning a transfer. It is not exhaustive. Before acting, verify later amendments, current practice and the requirements of the receiving bank or authority.

  1. Bangladesh Bank, FEID Circular No. 01, 8 March 2026 — paragraphs 1–3, valuation requirements in paragraph 5 and the annexures. Unlisted-company transfer and repatriation framework.
  2. Companies Act 1994, section 38 — transfer instrument, signature verification, company registration and articles-based restrictions, including subsection (3A) inserted in 2020.
  3. RJSC, Form 117 and RJSC filing guidance — read with the current submission checklist.
  4. HCCH, entry into force for Bangladesh, Convention status table and objections concerning Bangladesh’s accession — check the country pair and document.
  5. Finance Act 2022, section 3, substituted Stamp Act Schedule I, entry 61(a) — published share-transfer duty entry. Also consult the Stamp Act and subsequent fiscal amendments for the instrument’s execution date.
  6. Income Tax Act 2023, authentic English text published in October 2025 — sections 26–27 and 119. Read with subsequent income-tax amendments, applicable finance legislation, rules and any relevant treaty. Check the rate and any relief applicable to the transaction.
  7. Companies Act 1994, section 39 — certification of transfers and its legal effect.
