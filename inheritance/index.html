How to use this calculator

First time? Start here.

You do not need to know the fractions—the calculator works them out.

  1. Choose the applicable rulesUse the Bangladesh profile, or choose classical Islamic rules to compare methods.
  2. Enter what is left behindAdd money and any debts. Open land or special settings only when relevant.
  3. Count the familyEnter the relatives alive when the person died, then calculate.

Every relationship is to the person who died—not to you. Leave a count at 0 when that relative was not alive.

06

Will / bequest

After costs and debts

A bequest is a gift directed by a will. Enter a fraction such as 1/3; it is taken after costs and debts. If the gift is for an heir, select that in Special circumstances.

07

Family details

Count relatives who were alive at the time of death. Use + and − or type a number. Father means the deceased’s father; son means the deceased’s son.

What do the relationship names mean?

Full brother or sister: the same father and mother. Paternal half-sibling: the same father, different mother. Maternal half-sibling: the same mother, different father. All are relationships to the person who died.

For Bangladesh, enter grandchildren once in their predeceased parent’s family below. Other descendant situations require family-tree review.

Calculation methods, sources & limitations

This independent educational version covers prescribed shares, common blocking rules, residuaries, awl, configurable radd, Umariyyah, Mushtarakah and specified grandfather–sibling cases including Akdariyyah. It offers 67 relative categories for recording the family.

This is not an implementation of every case. Selected distant-kindred rules are implemented with explicit branch information where needed. Unborn-child and uncertain-eligibility outcomes are conditional. Multiple relationships, successive estates, multi-child statutory branch divisions and bequests to heirs require individual review. English and Bengali interfaces are provided; detailed rule explanations remain in English.

For Bangladesh, review section 4 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance, 1961 where a child of the deceased died earlier leaving children. The Bangladesh profile now calculates section 4 branch totals from the predeceased-child entries. See the statutory coverage and limits ↗.
